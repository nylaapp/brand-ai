# Writing quality: make it read like the brand, not like the top ten results

Generic content with nothing specific to say is what reads as machine-written. The fix is not word choice. A post reads as human when it contains things only this brand could know. Apply this at the brief (Step 2) and while writing (Step 3), and use it in the judgment pass (Step 6).

## 1. Build around first-hand material
Every post is built around **one first-hand asset** only the brand has: a founder story, a testing note, a customer question that keeps coming up, a mistake the brand made, sales or return data, a photo they took. Name it in the brief (`first_hand_asset`) and write around it.
- No asset available? Guided: ask for it (the "Your own insights" group). Automated: put a `[[BRAND TO ADD: …]]` placeholder where it belongs, finish the draft, and say at the top of the summary that it can't be published until the placeholder is filled.
- Never invent the asset. A post assembled from the top search results only restates what's already out there.

## 2. Have a point of view
Record a `stance` in the brief: the position the post takes and who it is *not* for ("we stopped recommending X because…", "most guides say Y; in our experience Z"). Use mild opinions and trade-offs. Skip the hedge-everything tone ("it depends on many factors").
- The stance comes from brand.md, the user, or first-hand material. Don't make one up. If there is none, ask (guided) or use a placeholder (automated) and say so.
- Regulated or health topics: a point of view never overrides claim limits or sources.

## 3. Be specific
Replace vague claims with numbers, names, places, times and materials ("tested for six weeks on…" rather than "high-quality formula"). One concrete example or small story per section. If you lack the detail, ask or placeholder it; never pad, never invent figures.

## 4. Write the way the brand talks to a customer
Contractions, short sentences, the customer's own words. Vary sentence length. One voice throughout ("we" for the brand, "you" for the reader). If a line isn't something you'd say aloud, rewrite it. Use bullets for parallel items and numbered lists for steps and rankings, and prose where prose is better; don't turn every section into a heading plus five equal bullets, and don't write long runs of paragraphs where a list would help the reader.

## 5. Avoid the usual tells (guidance, not a scored list)
- Filler openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world…", "Whether you're a beginner or a pro…", "In conclusion…", "Let's dive in."
- Stock words: unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, delve, tapestry, journey, "it's important to note."
- Patterns to vary: "It's not just X, it's Y"; lists of exactly three; a tidy summary closing every section; an em dash in every paragraph; uniform section structure.
Don't swap banned words for synonyms. Fix the cause: add the specific detail or the opinion.

## Judgment pass (Step 6)
Before delivery ask: What does this post say that a top-ten result doesn't? Where is the first-hand asset? What's the stance? Does each section have a concrete example? Would the brand say this out loud? Fix or placeholder what fails, and say in the summary what still needs the human.

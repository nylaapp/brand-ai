# Where to verify what

Use the regulator or the original publisher, not a secondary summary. Web addresses change; if one fails, search for the database by name. Always record the URL and the date you checked.

## What settles which question

| Question | Source that settles it |
|---|---|
| Is this drug or biologic approved, and for what? | The regulator's drug database and the official label |
| Is this device cleared, approved or granted, and for what use? | The regulator's device databases (clearance, approval, De Novo, classification) and the device's indications for use |
| Is this facility registered or this product listed? | The regulator's registration and listing records (and remember: not approval) |
| What are a product's indications, warnings, pregnancy and breastfeeding statements? | The official product label |
| Is this ingredient allowed, restricted or banned in this market? | The market's cosmetic-ingredient or food-additive database and regulations |
| Does this study exist and say that? | The paper itself (PubMed or the publisher), not a summary of it |
| Is there trial evidence? | The clinical trial registry |
| Is this safe in pregnancy or while breastfeeding? | The product's label, then a professional body or an expert service (see below) |
| Does this supplement ingredient have evidence? | A national health institute's supplement fact sheet, then primary studies |
| Is this advertising claim allowed? | The advertising regulator's guidance |
| Is this certification real? | The certifying body's own public directory |

## United States

- **Drugs, biologics:** Drugs@FDA (`accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/`), DailyMed for official labels (`dailymed.nlm.nih.gov`), FDA's Purple Book for biologics.
- **Devices:** FDA 510(k) database (`accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm`), Premarket Approval (PMA) database, De Novo database, Device Classification database, Establishment Registration and Device Listing.
- **Cosmetics:** FDA cosmetics pages (what the FDA does and doesn't regulate, and the cosmetics law), cosmetic ingredient safety resources, Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) for ingredient safety assessments.
- **Supplements:** NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets (`ods.od.nih.gov`), FDA dietary supplement guidance.
- **Advertising and endorsements:** FTC guidance on health product claims and its Endorsement Guides (`ftc.gov`).
- **Research:** PubMed and PubMed Central (`pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`), ClinicalTrials.gov, Cochrane reviews.
- **Public health and pregnancy:** CDC, MedlinePlus, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), MotherToBaby (pregnancy and breastfeeding exposures), LactMed (drugs and breastfeeding).

## Other markets (use the one that matches the content)

- **EU:** European Commission CosIng (cosmetic ingredients), EUDAMED and notified-body listings for CE-marked devices, EMA for medicines.
- **UK:** MHRA (medicines and devices), UK cosmetics rules.
- **Canada:** Health Canada's drug product database and medical device licence listing, Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist.
- **Australia:** TGA Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).
- **Philippines:** FDA Philippines product verification.
- **Elsewhere:** find the national medicines/device/cosmetics regulator and use its public register. If you can't find one, say so and mark the claim "cannot verify".

## Source quality

| Use as the source | Use only to find a source | Don't rely on |
|---|---|---|
| Regulator databases and labels; peer-reviewed papers; trial registries; professional bodies; national health institutes | Review articles, news coverage, Wikipedia (follow its citations to the original) | Competitor or brand marketing, forums, social posts, AI-generated summaries, affiliate "review" sites |

## Notes on dates and drift

- Regulatory records and labels change: clearances are added, indications are updated, warnings are revised. Check current records, and state the date.
- Studies can be retracted or superseded: check the publication's page for notices when a claim leans on a single paper.
- A source that is old isn't wrong by default, but say how old it is when a figure may have changed.

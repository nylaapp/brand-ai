# Campaign page examples: what real brands do (checked 2026-11-17)

These are field notes from 16 brands' Black Friday and holiday pages, ten days before Black Friday 2026. Pages
change every year, so treat this as a dated snapshot. Open a page live before showing it to a client. Everything
here was seen on the brand's own page unless it says otherwise:
- **Secondhand:** a third-party article, not the brand's page.
- **Not verified:** couldn't be confirmed.

Paraphrased throughout; no brand copy is reused.

## Patterns for building a page

1. **The offer is the headline,** stated plainly (Brooklinen, Ridge). The pages that work put the number in the
   first screen. AG1 shows the anti-pattern: the offer only in the bar, with a hero about something else.
2. **The saving shows on every card.** Show the crossed-out price, the new price and a percent chip, plus the star
   rating and review count (Brooklinen, Sephora). Kits and sets get a "($X value)" line (Sephora, Glossier).
3. **Tiles before grids on big catalogs.** Category tiles that each carry their own offer ("up to 50% off", "buy 2
   get 1") or a price floor ("from $24") let a shopper choose fast (Ridge, Gymshark, Samsung). A small catalog can
   go straight to a product grid of 6 to 8.
4. **The hero has jump links** to the deal groups, or two buttons by audience (Samsung, Gymshark). Still one main
   action per audience.
5. **Bundles get their own block or menu item:** build your own set, buy 2 get 1, buy 2+ save more (BEIS, Ridge,
   Samsung, Brooklinen, Death Wish).
6. **A trust row of three or four promises,** usually warranty, trial or returns, shipping and financing. The
   extended holiday returns window sits right under the hero (Sleepme, Ridge, Gymshark).
7. **The FAQ answers "when" first,** then students and members, delivery and returns (Samsung, Gymshark).
8. **Coming soon still sells** (BEIS, Glossier): a live early offer or bundle, a wishlist, "notify me" on products,
   and a signup with a reason to join.
9. **Each signup has its own reward** (Gymshark, Death Wish). Gamified reveal pop-ups (Ridge) and exit pop-ups
   (Sleepme) are used by the big players, but only fit when every box is a real deal, and they add a script. Never
   by default.
10. **Keep the URL all year.** Gymshark keeps its page up. Sephora redirects to /sale. Patagonia's old page is a
    404, and Delsey puts the year in its URLs. The working pattern is a URL with no year, kept published, that
    switches to an "ended" or "coming soon" state. Samsung's "see you next year" page is a much-cited example but
    could not be verified.
11. **No discount is a valid campaign** for some brands (REI, Patagonia, Apple). A values-led or gift-led page
    keeps the same structure, with the story in place of the offer and service facts (delivery, returns) in the
    first screen.
12. **A countdown in the bar** is the lightest urgency, and it travels to every page (AG1). It must count to the
    real end.

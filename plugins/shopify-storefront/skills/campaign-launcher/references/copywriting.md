# Writing campaign copy that sounds like the brand

The owner judges this skill by its words first. Correct facts in generic sale-speak is a failure: it reads like any
store's template. Every line should be something only this brand would write.

## 1. Load the voice before writing a word

Read all of `BRAND-VOICE.md` (or the voice section of the brand file the owner gave you), not just the three words:
- **the three words**: every line must fit all three;
- **How it sounds**: sentence length, we/you, humour level, how it handles urgency;
- **Signature phrases**: the brand's own turns of phrase. Reuse their rhythm and attitude, quote one where it fits;
- **Do / don't pairs** and **words to avoid**: treat every "don't" as a hard ban.

Then collect the brand's own words for this campaign, in this order: the owner's campaign materials (text on the
graphic or video, the brief), the live site's current headlines and product lines, the signature phrases. Start from
these. The owner already approved them.

## 2. Draft three directions, then pick

For the hero (headline, supporting line, button) and the announcement bar, write **three versions with clearly
different angles**:
1. **The brand's wink:** its humour or attitude applied to the offer.
2. **Product-led:** the hero product or the ritual it serves, then the offer.
3. **Plain offer:** the cleanest statement of the deal, still in the brand's rhythm.

Recommend one, and show the other two as alternatives in the plan (one line each). The owner picks faster from
three than they can describe what's wrong with one.

## 3. Lengths

| Surface | Length |
|---|---|
| Announcement bar | one line, at most about 60 characters on a phone |
| Hero headline | 2 to 5 words |
| Hero supporting line | one sentence, at most about 12 words, with the offer and the dates |
| Button | 2 or 3 specific words ("Shop the sale", "Get yours"), never "Learn more" or "Click here" |
| Email subject | under 45 characters |
| SMS | under 160 characters including the link |

## 4. Never write

- Generic sale-speak: "Don't miss out", "Shop now and save", "Limited time only", "Huge savings", "Treat yourself",
  "You deserve it", "Biggest sale of the year" (unless true and sourced).
- Filler verbs and AI tells: "unlock", "elevate", "indulge", "discover", "curated", "game-changer", "level up".
- Stacked exclamation marks, all caps beyond what the brand already uses, emojis the brand doesn't use, em dashes.
- Anything BRAND-VOICE.md lists under words or habits to avoid, and anything Claim limits forbids.

## 5. Check every line before showing it

For each line of copy, answer in your head and rewrite any line that fails:
1. **Swap test:** put a competitor's name on it. If it still works, it's too generic. Add the brand's product,
   ritual, attitude or one of its phrases.
2. **Three words:** does it fit each of the brand's three words?
3. **Do / don't:** does it break any "don't" pair?
4. **Facts:** offer, dates, exclusions and link match the plan exactly ("sitewide", not "everything", when gift cards
   are excluded).
5. **Read aloud:** does it sound like the owner talking, or like an ad template?

In the plan, under the copy, add one line saying which signature phrase or voice rule each recommended line leans
on, so the owner sees why it's on-brand.
